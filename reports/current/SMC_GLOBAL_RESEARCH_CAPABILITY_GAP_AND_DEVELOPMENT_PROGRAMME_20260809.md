## Executive verdict

SMC Codex Desk has built the difficult part that many trading projects skip: deterministic evidence ownership, closed-candle truth, explicit provenance, multi-timeframe structure, fail-closed behavior, abstention, immutable review artifacts, and a governance boundary that refuses to call unproven research a trading edge.

Its main weakness is now equally clear. The Desk is much better at recognizing **chart grammar** than demonstrating **market mechanism**. It can observe that price penetrated a geometric level and reclaimed it. With candles alone, it cannot truthfully conclude that a stop cluster was triggered, that an institution accumulated, that hidden liquidity absorbed flow, or that a fair-value gap represents an order-book imbalance. Those are separate hypotheses requiring trades, quotes, depth, cancellations, derivative state, cross-venue context, and outcome tests.

The right next move is therefore not another broad rewrite, more AI narration, or premature prediction work. The work order is:

1. Complete the already-defined blind human perception gate.
2. Resolve doctrine disagreements before changing detector thresholds.
3. Freeze a claim-boundary vocabulary separating geometry, mechanism inference, and forecast.
4. Add a read-only microstructure evidence sidecar without displacing the canonical OHLCV spine.
5. Test whether SMC-shaped events correspond to measurable mechanisms using predeclared event studies and matched controls.
6. Add venue/regime transfer, calibrated forecasting, and human-AI utility testing only after the earlier gates pass.
7. Reopen economic edge research last, under trial accounting, multiple-testing correction, realistic costs, and untouched future evidence.

This ordering joins the original ambition—an AI professional SMC colleague—with what the failed alpha work and current governance have taught: first make perception reproducibly correct, then make causal language empirically honest, then ask whether the combined human-plus-AI system makes better decisions, and only then ask whether any result survives costs as an executable edge.

## 2. What the international evidence actually says

### 2.1 Chart patterns can be formalized; labels are not self-validating

Lo, Mamaysky, and Wang showed that subjective chart patterns can be translated into computational definitions and tested for conditional information in a historical U.S. equity sample. That is an important precedent for the Desk's deterministic geometry, but it does not validate SMC as a branded ontology or establish a causal story for an order block, fair-value gap, sweep, BOS, or CHoCH. [NBER working paper](https://www.nber.org/papers/w7613)

The research implication is narrow and useful: a discretionary visual language can become falsifiable when definitions, cases, outcomes, and statistical tests are frozen in advance.

### 2.2 Real short-horizon price formation is strongly tied to book events and available depth

Cont, Kukanov, and Stoikov found that short-interval price changes in their U.S. stock sample were strongly related to order-flow imbalance and that price impact varied inversely with depth. Sirignano and Cont then found generalizable predictive information in billions of NASDAQ orders, quotes, and cancellations. Farmer and coauthors found that large price moves in their studied order book were associated more with liquidity gaps than exceptional market-order size. [Order-flow imbalance study](https://academic.oup.com/jfec/article-abstract/12/1/47/816163), [universal order-book features](https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.06917), [liquidity-gap study](https://arxiv.org/abs/cond-mat/0312703)

Together, these studies undermine a common shortcut: a large candle is not itself evidence of large institutional aggression. It may reflect aggressive flow, thin depth, withdrawn liquidity, cascades, cross-market repricing, or several mechanisms at once. The Desk's OHLCV displacement detector is a valid chart-shape detector; it is not yet an order-flow detector.

### 2.3 Stop clusters and cascades are plausible, but manipulation is not observed from a wick

Osler's FX research found stop-loss clustering near round numbers and faster, larger, more persistent moves after relevant trigger regions in the studied currencies. This supports the plausibility of clustered conditional liquidity and cascades. It does not establish that every wick beyond equal highs is a stop hunt, identify the triggering participants, or prove deliberate institutional manipulation. [Stop-loss cascade paper](https://www.newyorkfed.org/medialibrary/media/research/staff_reports/sr150.pdf)

The Desk may defensibly label a level penetration and close-back-inside pattern. It should call the causal interpretation a hypothesis until trade, book, recovery, continuation, and preferably cross-market evidence support it.

### 2.4 Price-only evidence is incomplete

Blume, Easley, and O'Hara showed why volume can carry information not present in price alone, while market-microstructure research more generally treats informed activity as latent and inferred under explicit assumptions rather than visibly stamped on a candle. [Price and volume research](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/j.1540-6261.1994.tb04424.x), [Kyle's continuous-auction model](https://econpapers.repec.org/article/ecmemetrp/v_3a53_3ay_3a1985_3ai_3a6_3ap_3a1315-35.htm)

This supports the Desk's deterministic ownership model but requires more careful wording. Deterministic candle facts can own geometry. Mechanism inference must be probabilistic and evidence-bounded.

### 2.5 Market design changes what a chart event means

The United States is fragmented across exchanges and non-exchange execution venues. China uses market-specific call and continuous auctions, price-time priority, tick constraints, and board-specific daily price limits. Japan uses Itayose and Zaraba mechanisms, daily limits, special quote states, and a high-speed exchange system with explicit auction behavior. Binance perpetual futures trade continuously but add funding, leverage, liquidation, contract-state, and cross-exchange fragmentation. [SEC market-structure overview](https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/speeches-statements/us-equity-market-structure), [Shanghai Stock Exchange mechanism](https://english.sse.com.cn/start/trading/mechanism/), [JPX transaction methods](https://www.jpx.co.jp/english/equities/trading/domestic/04.html), [JPX arrowhead](https://www.jpx.co.jp/english/systems/equities-trading/01.html), [cryptocurrency futures microstructure](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/fut.22305)

A chart-shaped sweep around a Chinese daily limit, a Japanese special quote or call auction, a U.S. fragmented NBBO, and a Binance liquidation cascade are not the same event. Cross-market transfer therefore requires a market-design ontology before model transfer.

### 2.6 Large rule searches create convincing false winners

The Japanese evidence is especially useful as a warning. A study testing 7,846 intraday technical rules reported that none beat the market after data-snooping adjustment. White's Reality Check, Sullivan–Timmermann–White, the Probability of Backtest Overfitting, and the Deflated Sharpe Ratio all address different parts of the same problem: choosing the best result after many trials inflates apparent evidence. [Japanese intraday rule study](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0378426612001756), [Reality Check](https://doi.org/10.1111/1468-0262.00152), [data-snooping bootstrap](https://doi.org/10.1111/0022-1082.00163), [PBO](https://scholarworks.wmich.edu/math_pubs/42/), [Deflated Sharpe Ratio](https://www.davidhbailey.com/dhbpapers/deflated-sharpe.pdf)

The previous Desk alpha search should be treated as part of the research trial universe, not forgotten when a future model is evaluated. A new winning configuration cannot be interpreted as trial one.

### 2.7 Confidence must be an evaluated forecast, not a linguistic style

Proper probabilistic forecasting requires calibration and sharpness evaluated with proper scoring rules. Regime shifts complicate pooled calibration because a model can appear acceptable on average while failing in a particular state. [Proper scoring rules](https://doi.org/10.1198/016214506000001437), [probabilistic forecasting framework](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=2405902), [Hamilton regime switching](https://ideas.repec.org/a/ecm/emetrp/v57y1989i2p357-84.html), [Bayesian online change-point detection](https://arxiv.org/abs/0710.3742)

The Desk's existing calibration, OOD, abstention, competing-risk, and walk-forward modules are promising research scaffolding. They are not integrated into the canonical colleague, are not validated on a frozen event dataset, and should not inherit authority from their code existence.

### 2.8 A persuasive AI explanation can make the team worse

Human-AI studies show that explanations and confidence can influence acceptance and trust without reliably producing complementary team performance. That is directly relevant to an articulate SMC colleague: fluent narratives may encourage acceptance even when the underlying answer is wrong. [Explanation and complementary performance study](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.14779), [confidence and trust-calibration study](https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.02114), [NIST AI Risk Management Framework](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/ai/NIST.AI.100-1.pdf)

The relevant unit of success is therefore not only system label accuracy. It is whether analyst plus Desk outperforms analyst alone, with better calibrated reliance and no unacceptable increase in catastrophic errors.

### 2.9 Vision remains an audit channel, not truth ownership

Chart-question-answering research separates value extraction, visual grounding, and logical reasoning. RefChartQA supports explicit grounding, while ChartQA illustrates that visual extraction and reasoning are distinct failure points. Neither benchmark certifies candlestick or SMC competence. [RefChartQA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.23131), [ChartQA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.10244)

The Desk's current policy—deterministic data owns facts, vision audits—remains correct.

## 3. Scientific translation of the SMC vocabulary

The following crosswalk should become the claim boundary for future work.

| SMC term | Defensible chart observation | Possible measurable mechanism | Current Desk evidence | Required evidence before mechanism claim |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Liquidity pool | Repeated or clustered swing prices; equal highs/lows | Clustered conditional orders, resting depth, participant focal points | OHLCV geometry and swing clustering | Trades, book depth, cancellations, trigger-time flow, recovery path, cross-market state |
| Sweep | Price crosses a frozen reference and closes/reclaims according to a fixed rule | Stops triggered, liquidity consumed, spoofed/withdrawn depth, liquidation cascade, information shock | Candle penetration/reclaim and structural context | Signed trade flow, OFI, depth depletion/replenishment, liquidation data, continuation/reversal outcome |
| Displacement | Bar or sequence exceeds a frozen range/body/ATR rule | Aggressive imbalance, thin book, liquidity withdrawal, forced liquidation, cross-venue repricing | Candle range/body/close geometry and aggregate exchange volume fields | Event-level trades, multi-level depth, spread, cancellations, liquidation/derivative state, leader-lagger context |
| Fair-value gap | Frozen three-candle non-overlap geometry | Rapid repricing or low two-sided trading in a price interval | OHLCV interval geometry | Traded-volume-at-price and order-book evidence; do not equate automatically with a book gap |
| Order block | Frozen candle/zone preceding a qualifying structural move | Candidate origin region for inventory, absorption, latent liquidity, or simply a historical pivot | Causal-origin chart heuristic and later reaction | Event-study effect versus matched controls; trade/book behavior at origin and revisit; falsifiable invalidation |
| BOS | Frozen close beyond a confirmed structural reference | State transition with possible information or flow continuation | Deterministic house structure graph | Expert agreement plus conditional-outcome evidence; retain as house label, not universal fact |
| CHoCH/MSS | Frozen first opposing structural transition under a state rule | Possible regime or control change | Deterministic house-state transition | Expert agreement, change-point/regime evidence, and prospective outcome behavior |
| Absorption | Repeated attempts with limited price progress | Passive liquidity replenishment against aggressive flow | Not observable from canonical OHLCV | Signed aggressive flow plus stable price, depth replenishment/cancellation behavior, venue sequence integrity |
| Institutional activity | None directly | Latent informed or large-participant behavior | Not directly observed | Participant identifiers where lawful, size/flow models, impact signatures, alternative explanations, uncertainty |

Three language changes follow immediately:

- Use **chart liquidity proxy** for geometric level clusters unless order/stop evidence exists.
- Use **candle fair-value-gap geometry** rather than **order-book imbalance** unless book data supports the latter.
- Use **candidate causal origin zone** rather than **institutional order block** until matched-control and microstructure evidence exists.

These changes do not weaken SMC Codex Desk. They make every claim more exact and therefore more testable.

## 6. Exact development programme

### Gate R0 — Claim-boundary freeze

**Purpose:** Prevent unsupported causal language from outrunning evidence.

**Work:**

1. Add the scientific crosswalk in Section 3 to doctrine as a versioned vocabulary proposal.
2. Tag every field in future evidence packs as one of: observed geometry, derived chart state, mechanism hypothesis, outcome forecast, or action recommendation.
3. Ban unqualified phrases such as institutional accumulation, order-flow absorption, engineered stop hunt, and smart-money entry when the evidence is only OHLCV.

**Pass condition:** Every user-visible claim can be traced to a named evidence class, and the system refuses to upgrade geometry into mechanism.

**Authority after pass:** No change; research/observe-only.

### Gate R1 — Human truth first

**Purpose:** Establish whether the Desk detects the intended chart language before adding new data or models.

**Work:**

1. Independent analyst selects 12–15 development cases blind to system answers.
2. Seal definition_set_status_v2 and build the immutable cohort.
3. One expert marks every case using markup_annotation_v2.
4. Score the system only after the cohort and answers are sealed.
5. Classify every disagreement as detector error, doctrine ambiguity, adapter/display error, insufficient context, or expert inconsistency.

**Pass condition:** Cohort-integrity checks pass and a complete disagreement ledger exists. Do not invent a headline accuracy threshold before seeing the error distribution.

**Authority after pass:** Still research-only. This is development evidence, not a blind certificate.

### Gate R2 — Doctrine reliability

**Purpose:** Turn contested SMC labels into reproducible house definitions.

**Work:**

1. Use the R1 disagreement ledger to select the minimum doctrine changes.
2. Freeze sweep significance, reference ownership, decisive-close rules, FVG eligibility, order-block origin, and BOS/CHoCH precedence.
3. Have a second qualified reviewer mark a stratified contest set.
4. Use label-appropriate agreement: exact agreement for categorical states, interval overlap/distance for zones and prices, and event matching with a declared tolerance for structures.
5. Adjudicate disagreements without showing either reviewer the system's preferred answer.

**Pass condition:** Definitions are versioned, contested examples are included, and inter-rater results identify both reliable and unreliable labels.

**Authority after pass:** Perception claims may be promoted label by label only; no causal, forecast, or trade authority.

### Gate R3 — Read-only mechanism evidence sidecar

**Purpose:** Add the observables needed to test mechanism claims while preserving the canonical candle system.

**Architecture:**

```text
canonical completed candles ──────────────┐
                                          ├─> versioned event evidence pack
sequenced trades + L2 book + derivatives ┘
                 │
                 └─> integrity monitor, never silent fallback
```

**Work:**

1. Integrate the existing aggregate-trade adapter as read-only evidence, not as a signal.
2. Add Binance depth snapshot plus sequenced delta ingestion with explicit gap detection and resynchronization.
3. Add mark/index price, funding, open interest, basis, and liquidation events where the source and history are trustworthy.
4. Persist immutable event windows around every frozen SMC event.
5. Record source, venue, contract, sequence range, clock skew, missingness, and reconstruction status.
6. Fail closed on sequence gaps; never backfill a mechanism claim from candle shape.

**Pass condition:** Replay produces the same event state; injected sequence gaps are detected; every field has provenance; the sidecar can be disabled without changing canonical chart labels.

**Authority after pass:** Evidence display only.

### Gate R4 — Proxy-validity experiments

**Purpose:** Test whether chart labels correspond to the mechanisms commonly attributed to them.

**Work:**

1. Predeclare one hypothesis per label. Example: qualifying displacement events have greater normalized OFI and lower pre-event depth than matched non-displacement bars.
2. Define primary outcomes before viewing results.
3. Match controls on time of week, volatility, volume, spread, regime, trend state, and event opportunity.
4. Compare clock-time and event/volume-time windows.
5. Measure pre-event, trigger, immediate recovery, and later continuation paths.
6. Report effect distributions and uncertainty, not only p-values.
7. Correct for all attempted labels, thresholds, horizons, and subgroups.

**Minimum experiments:**

| SMC hypothesis | Primary mechanism outcome | Important falsifier |
|---|---|---|
| Sweep events consume clustered liquidity then reclaim | Trigger-window OFI/depth depletion followed by replenishment and bounded recovery | Same pattern occurs in controls without unusual flow/depth behavior |
| Displacement reflects one-sided price formation | OFI, spread/depth change, book gap, liquidation share | Large candles are explained by ordinary volatility or cross-venue repricing |
| FVG marks low two-sided trading | Volume-at-price deficit and/or transient book gap through the interval | Interval traded normally despite candle non-overlap |
| Order-block revisit has special response | Conditional response and microstructure behavior versus matched zones | No incremental effect versus ordinary pivots/support-resistance controls |
| CHoCH/MSS indicates regime transition | Change-point/regime posterior and prospective state persistence | Label adds no information beyond simple break/volatility controls |

**Pass condition:** At least one frozen-label experiment is reproduced on a later untouched period, with the full trial ledger disclosed.

**Authority after pass:** Supported mechanism hypotheses may be described probabilistically; failed hypotheses must lose causal language.

### Gate R5 — Venue and regime transfer

**Purpose:** Determine whether the system learned a robust abstraction or a Binance-specific visual habit.

**Order:**

1. Binance BTCUSDT spot versus perpetual.
2. Same instrument on an independent crypto venue.
3. Additional crypto instruments with frozen definitions.
4. Only then design separate China and Japan studies under their market rules and licensed data.

**Work:** Build the market-design fields in Section 4, estimate per-venue effects, test drift/change points, and publish failure slices. Never pool incompatible auction/limit/contract states merely to increase sample size.

**Pass condition:** Geometry and mechanism effects are reported separately by venue and regime, with explicit non-transfer cases.

**Authority after pass:** Bounded to the tested venue, instrument, rule version, and regime coverage.

### Gate R6 — Calibrated decision support

**Purpose:** Convert supported evidence into honest uncertainty about frozen outcomes.

**Work:**

1. Define competing outcomes such as target first, invalidation first, neither by horizon, and ambiguous/data-failed.
2. Use rolling-origin evaluation with train/calibration/test separation and purging where windows overlap.
3. Compare simple empirical baselines before complex models.
4. Report Brier/log score, calibration curves, sharpness, class balance, risk-coverage curves, and per-regime results.
5. Calibrate target and invalidation jointly or with a coherent competing-risk model; avoid independently calibrated probabilities that are merely renormalized without validation.
6. Require abstention under OOD, sparse support, data gaps, or unstable calibration.
7. Attach confidence intervals and minimum-support disclosures to every slice.

**Pass condition:** A later untouched test period shows calibration and decision-relevant improvement over simple baselines, with no unacceptable regime-specific failure hidden by the pooled score.

**Authority after pass:** Forecast research only until a separate governance promotion.

### Gate R7 — Human-AI complementarity

**Purpose:** Prove that the market colleague helps the person, not merely that it speaks convincingly.

**Design:** Counterbalanced cases with analyst alone, Desk alone, and analyst plus Desk. Randomize case order and hide future outcomes. Separate initial judgment from post-advice judgment.

**Metrics:**

- Perception and outcome accuracy.
- Time to a committed judgment.
- Correct and incorrect override rates.
- Appropriate reliance conditional on Desk correctness.
- Calibration of the human's final confidence.
- Catastrophic miss and false-certainty rates.
- Explanatory traceability and unsupported-claim rate.

**Pass condition:** The combined condition improves predeclared primary metrics without worsening catastrophic-error or false-certainty bounds.

**Authority after pass:** Decision-support usefulness within the tested workflow, not autonomous execution.

### Gate R8 — Economic validity

**Purpose:** Reopen the edge question only after perception, mechanism, forecast, and team utility are credible.

**Work:**

1. Register every strategy, threshold, feature, exit, cost, timeframe, and subgroup trial, including historical work.
2. Freeze the execution model: spread, taker/maker fees, slippage, impact, funding, latency, queue assumptions, and liquidation constraints.
3. Use realistic opportunity timestamps and no look-ahead in higher-timeframe context.
4. Apply multiple-testing/data-snooping controls and selection-adjusted performance.
5. Preserve an untouched future holdout and then a live shadow period.
6. Report capacity, tail loss, stability, and missed-trade behavior—not only average return or Sharpe.

**Pass condition:** Independent replication on untouched evidence after costs, with selection bias controlled and failure regimes disclosed.

**Authority after pass:** Still no execution. Paper and live authority require separate operational, safety, and governance gates.

## 9. Decisions that make the system cleaner

### Preserve one factual spine

Completed candles and deterministic derived facts remain canonical. Microstructure data joins as a sidecar with its own integrity state. The system must expose disagreement or missingness rather than merging sources silently.

### Separate three authorities

1. **Perception authority:** What happened geometrically?
2. **Mechanism authority:** What observable market process is supported?
3. **Forecast/action authority:** What may happen next, and what—if anything—should be done?

Passing one never promotes the next automatically.

### Promote labels independently

BOS may become reliable while order-block origin remains ambiguous. Sweep geometry may be reliable while the stop-cascade explanation fails. The certification unit should therefore be label × market × timeframe × definition version × evidence layer, not a single global badge.

### Prefer null results to narrative repair

If a chart label shows no incremental mechanism or outcome information versus matched controls, keep it as descriptive chart grammar or remove its causal language. Do not rescue it by changing the horizon, subgroup, or threshold without recording a new trial.

## 10. Success definition

The improved SMC Codex Desk is not the system that knows the most SMC words or produces the boldest calls. It is the system that can answer, for every statement:

1. What exactly was observed?
2. Which versioned rule derived it?
3. Which causal interpretation is supported, and which alternatives remain?
4. How often has this claim agreed with independent experts?
5. How has it behaved on untouched data, by venue and regime?
6. Is its uncertainty calibrated?
7. Does the human-plus-Desk team make better decisions?
8. What authority has actually been earned?

That path is narrower than building another impressive trading demo, but it is the credible route to the professional market colleague the project is trying to become.

## Appendix A — Evidence boundary

This review prioritizes primary research, official exchange rules, and official risk/governance frameworks. Commercial education, influencer doctrine, and vendor articles were excluded as empirical authority. They may describe how a community uses a term; they cannot establish that the term identifies a market mechanism or profitable effect.

This search did not locate a primary empirical paper validating the branded SMC/ICT ontology as a unified causal or profitable trading method. That is a bounded review finding, not proof of universal absence. It is sufficient reason to test the ontology component by component instead of assuming its causal claims.

## Appendix B — Research quality rules

- Freeze definitions and outcomes before inspecting performance.
- Preserve raw data, transformations, sequence integrity, and source versions.
- Record every attempted hypothesis, parameter, horizon, feature, and subgroup.
- Use purged chronological evaluation where event windows overlap.
- Separate development, calibration, blind test, and live shadow evidence.
- Compare against simple controls and ordinary technical-analysis alternatives.
- Report effect size, uncertainty, coverage, missingness, and failure slices.
- Make negative and contradictory evidence first-class outputs.
- Never allow a model, narrative, or UI layer to upgrade its own authority.
